Privacy policy

Name Game: Mutual Friends · Effective November 26, 2026

The short version. The app has no servers and no accounts. I don't collect, receive or store your contacts or any other personal information. Your contact list stays on your iPhone.

Who I am

The app is made by Eric Macy in Jackson, Wyoming. Questions about this policy: ebhmacy@mac.com.

What the app asks to use

  • Contacts. The app reads the phone numbers in your contacts only while a check is running, to find friends you have in common. It never saves your whole contact list.
  • Camera. Used to scan the QR code on your friend's phone. Nothing is photographed or saved.
  • Bluetooth. Used to connect to the nearby phone whose code you scanned.

How a check works

Each phone scrambles its phone numbers with a secret key that only that phone knows. The two phones swap their scrambled lists over Bluetooth and scramble them again. Only numbers that were on both lists end up looking identical, so those are the only numbers either phone can recognize. Everything sent between the phones is encrypted with a key from the QR code, so only the phone that scanned it can join. Each code works once.

The result is that you and your friend each see the people you both know, and nothing else from each other's lists.

What leaves your phone

I receive nothing. The only data that leaves your phone is the encrypted, scrambled exchange with the one phone you scanned, over Bluetooth. The app sends nothing to me, to any server, or to any advertising or analytics company.

If you choose Share a problem report, the app opens the iPhone share sheet and you decide where the report goes. The report contains the activity log described below, your iOS version, the app's version and build, and a random tag that identifies the install but not you. It never contains names, phone numbers or contacts.

What the app saves on your phone

  • The people you chose to hide.
  • Your last 50 results: the names and numbers of the friends you had in common.
  • Whether you have seen the welcome screens.
  • A small private activity log: which screens were opened, whether a try worked, and how long it took. It is kept for up to 30 days. It never contains names, phone numbers or contacts.

This is kept in the app's private storage on your iPhone. The app doesn't add its own encryption on top of what iOS provides. To erase everything, open People I hide and choose Erase everything the app saved. Deleting the app does the same.

Limits you should know about

  • Your friend's phone learns roughly how many numbers you offered.
  • A deliberately modified copy of the app could test numbers it already knows against your list during a session. Scanning in person, one-use codes and a size limit make that hard to do at scale.
  • If the connection drops at the very end, one phone may see the result and the other may not.

Apple

If you install the app from the App Store or TestFlight, Apple handles the download and may collect information under its own privacy policy. If you choose to share crash reports or feedback with developers through your iPhone or TestFlight, I may receive that crash information and anything you write in your feedback. I never receive your contacts.

Children

The app doesn't collect personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes

If the app ever sends data to a server, I will change the app and this policy first, and ask for your permission before anything is sent. The date at the top shows when this policy last changed.
